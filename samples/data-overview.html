What the classes actually are

The two samples above are one class each out of nine. The full label space, and how unevenly it is populated, is the thing that shapes every number we report.

Vision — WM-811K, 9 classes, 9,357 held-out maps

One class is non-defective. None means no recognisable spatial pattern — not a perfect wafer. Scattered failures below the pattern threshold are still labelled None. That is WM-811K’s convention, not ours. The other eight are defect patterns.

Why we report macro-F1, not accuracy. None is 59.1% of the split, so “call every wafer clean” already scores 0.591 accuracy while catching nothing. Macro-F1 weights Near-full’s 22 maps the same as None’s 5,529, so a class the model abandons cannot hide in the average. Our number is 0.9232.

That 59.1% is already after subsampling — None was cut to 25% of available records during data prep. In raw WM-811K the non-defective class dominates so heavily the model simply learns to say None.

There is no binary head. The model predicts one of nine classes; anything that is not None is treated as defective downstream. A faint Local read as None is a miss that macro-F1 exposes and a pass/fail number would hide.

SECOM — binary, and far more skewed

No defect patterns here. SECOM records 1,567 production lots against 590 anonymised sensors, with one bit of outcome per lot.

LabelLotsShareMeaning
Pass (+1) 1,46393.4% lot cleared in-line test
Fail (−1) 1046.6% lot failed — a 1:14 ratio

A 20% stratified validation split leaves 293 passing and 21 failing lots. Twenty-one positives is the entire budget the detector is scored on.

Validation, n=314Predicted passPredicted fail
Actual pass (293)26825 false alarms
Actual fail (21)15 missed6 caught

Fail-class recall 0.286, precision 0.194, ROC-AUC 0.583. We do not report accuracy — it is ~87% and means nothing at 1:14. Predicting “all pass” scores 0% recall, so 28.6% is a real gain over the do-nothing baseline, and still a weak detector.

Eight sweeps over 184 configurations did not move this, and a supervised hybrid made it worse (recall 0.190). The ceiling is the data: the typical failing lot is not distinguishable from a passing one by sensor magnitude — which is exactly what the 1.93σ vs 2.02σ comparison above shows. This is the measured reason Track A moved to PHM 2016 CMP.

Where this sits in the manufacturing flow

A logic wafer takes 600–1,000+ steps across 40–80 mask layers, but they are repetitions of a small set. Below is the fleet this system actually reasons over, and the steps it does not.

Two of the six are not process steps. A handler moves wafers and a filter cleans air, yet both are real sources of yield loss — which is the point. Not every excursion comes from a process tool.

Steps we do not cover

DepositionCVD / PVD / ALD, epitaxy — grow and add thin films
Ion implantationintroduce dopants into the silicon
Clean & thermalinter-step cleans, oxidation, anneal

The public data gives us no labelled examples of these. The architecture is step-agnostic — it reasons over sensors, patterns and precedents — but we do not claim coverage we have not evaluated. Deposition is the largest gap, and it is where most published virtual-metrology work concentrates.

Defect pattern → likely step

Wafer maps come from the end of the line, at wafer test: one pass/fail bin per die, after all processing. The pattern is a symptom observed last; inferring which of hundreds of upstream steps caused it is the actual problem.

Near-full is the interesting one. Near-total failure can be a genuine power-supply cascade, or a test-head calibration drift where the wafers are perfectly fine. That distinction decides whether you scrap a lot or recalibrate an instrument, and it is Case Study 6.

Read the sensor values against each other

The failing lot peaks at 1.93σ. The passing lot peaks at 2.02σ — the passing lot deviates more. That is not an error in the sample; it is what SECOM is, and it is why the anomaly detector reaches ROC-AUC 0.583 and fail-class recall 0.286 regardless of tuning.

The loudest failing row in SECOM peaks at 21.8σ. Using it would make the detector look decisive. Choosing the medoid instead is the difference between demonstrating a system and flattering one.

It is also why Track A moved to the PHM 2016 CMP dataset, where the process measurements and the outcome are recorded on the same wafer — the one place in this system where a sensor-to-outcome claim is measured rather than asserted. See the Prediction & abstention screen.
